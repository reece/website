## About Me

I am a scientist and engineering leader with over two decades of
experience building reliable software systems for research and
clinical genetic testing. I enjoy collaborating with smart people to solve complex problems and leading teams that create products that matter. I have a knack for expressing the big picture of a mission while
staying focused on incremental delivery of tangible value, and building the teams that make this possible.

I received degrees in molecular biology (BA) from
[UC San Diego](https://ucsd.edu/), early mapping of chromosome 11 for the nascent
Human Genome Project at the [Salk Institute](https://salk.edu/), joint program in
molecular biophysics (PhD) and computer science (MS) at Washington University in St. Louis.
This education was enabled by public funding through the [National Institutes of
Health](https://nih.gov/), [the National Science Foundation](https://nsf.gov/), and the [Department of Energy](https://doe.gov/). After my PhD, I worked on pattern
discovery in protein sequences at the [IBM Watson Research Center](https://research.ibm.com/labs/yorktown-heights).

As a scientist at [Genentech](https://www.gene.com/), I conceived of and built Unison, the protein
annotation system that was used by research, development, and legal teams for over a decade to
discover and analyze protein sequences. The system featured carefully versioned and indexed
feature predictions from over 60 sources and methods across essentially all known human and
non-human proteins, and contributed to numerous drug target development programs. Later, I oversaw
Research Computing Architecture and Operations, with a matrixed team of approximately 20 IT
professionals and an annual equipment and services budget of $30M. At [Invitae](https://www.invitae.com/), I
was among the very first employees who developed the laboratory methods and systems that
sequenced and delivered diagnostic genomics reports to over six million patients and their
clinicians before the company was acquired by LabCorp. In addition to product development and
engineering, I was directly involved in hiring and onboarding as the company scaled to roughly 750
by the time of my departure. As CTO at [MyOme](https://myome.com/), I built the engineering team, initiated the operations
team that received our first ~50 clinical samples, hired our first project managers, and
oversaw compliance efforts as our first HIPAA privacy and security officer. I was deeply
involved in the architecture and implementation of a system for clinical genetics test ordering,
processing, and interpretation that was nimble enough to adapt as the company found
product-market fit. I am the founder and executive director of [biocommons.org](http://biocommons.org),
a volunteer-run community that builds pre-competitive open source tools for genome interpretation
and that are trusted by multiple companies internationally for clinical genetics interpretation.

Throughout my career, I have endeavored to balancing product, scientific, and technical depth
while simultaneously building kind, enjoyable, focused, and effective teams. I think strategically
and have a bias toward action. I am the first author of the most recent update to the
[HGVS Nomenclature](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13073-024-01421-5), an internationally-recognized
standard for the description of DNA, RNA, and protein sequence variants. I also founded and
chaired the Variation Representation effort at the [Alliance for Genomics and Health](https://ga4gh.org/)
and implemented the first version of the [Variation Representation Specification](https://vrs.ga4gh.org/).
I have expertise with Linux, Python, PostgreSQL, AWS, GCP, CI/CD pipelines, data engineering and
pipelines, security, and devops. I am comfortable building scrappy solutions for quick answers and
resilient systems for mission-critical operations. I am a practiced, thoughtful, and skeptical user
of AI throughout product development, including brainstorming, competitive analyses, feature
prioritization, product roadmapping, system architecture, implementation planning, and, frontend
and backend implementation.

Over the last decade, I have become increasingly determined to use my time, skills, and
experience to improve political representation in the United States. In 2017, I began
volunteering with [States Win](https://stateswin.org/) (then known as Sister
District), including extensive canvassing in Arizona, co-organizing the canvassing effort in
2019, and leading a Virginia canvassing delegation that led to the VA trifecta. In 2020, I was
a core member of the small, passionate, and all-volunteer team that built [landslide2020.org](http://landslide2020.org),
a relational organizing platform to promote accountability among friends. In 2026, I help modernize
[Indivisible SF's website](https://indivisiblesf.org). I also built [No Kings Countdown](http://nokingscountdown.org/)
to motivate people leading up to No Kings 3 on March 28. In the course of building these tools, I
have used APIs and services from Alloy, Ballotpedia, Civic Engine, Congress.gov, and vote.org.
